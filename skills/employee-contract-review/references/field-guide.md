# Employee contract review — field guide

How to populate each review field. The template's own column headers govern the exact set — treat
this as the meaning behind common fields, and adapt to whatever columns are present. Read the
**current** position (after any variation) and note the original where it changed.

## The documents you'll see per employee

- **Statement of Main Terms of Employment** — the main contract (the standard template).
- **Variation / appraisal letter(s)** — later changes to salary, job title, hours, or pension.
  There can be more than one; apply them in date order.
- **Agreement on Obligations during and after Employment** — the addendum holding confidentiality,
  IP and the restrictive covenants.
- **Leaver documents** — resignation letter/email, resignation acceptance, leaver letter (only for
  employees who have left or are leaving).
- **Apprenticeship agreement** — a *different* contract type (fixed-term); see the deviations note.

## Standard clause wording (this employer reuses one template)

Once you've read one full set, most of these are identical across staff. State the standard
position and only elaborate where an individual deviates.

- **Type** — "Standard — 'Statement of Main Terms of Employment' + signed Obligations addendum",
  plus a note of any variations. Flag non-standard types (apprenticeship, fixed-term, bespoke).
- **Payment date** — salary paid monthly in arrears, on/around a fixed day of the month.
- **Commission & additional payments** — usually none; overtime compensated by time off in lieu
  (TOIL). Note any one-off bonuses mentioned in variation letters.
- **Pension** — auto-enrolment scheme meeting statutory minimums (employer ~3%). Note any employee
  who has varied their contribution rate.
- **Benefits** — often only an Employee Assistance Programme (EAP), stated to be non-contractual
  and withdrawable. Note the absence of car allowance / private medical / death-in-service if the
  template asks for benefits.
- **Holidays** — e.g. 25 days + 8 bank/public holidays (pro-rata for part-time), plus the holiday
  year. **Carry-over:** newer contracts may expressly forbid carrying days forward; older ones are
  often silent and defer to the Employee Handbook — say which, and that no set number of carry-over
  days is granted.
- **Sick Pay** — distinguish **statutory (SSP) only** from an **enhanced company scheme**. The
  enhanced scheme here is discretionary and service-related (e.g. 4 weeks' full pay for 1–2 years'
  service, 12 weeks' for 2+ years). Note it is at the company's "absolute discretion" (not a firm
  contractual entitlement) and tied to continuous service. Apprenticeship agreements are often
  SSP-only.
- **Restrictive Covenants** — from the Obligations addendum: a fixed period (e.g. 6 months)
  post-termination of non-solicitation and non-dealing with Restricted Customers, non-poaching and
  non-employment of Restricted Persons, and a non-compete (often UK-wide), reduced by garden leave.
  Define **Restricted Business** (the employer's business, or the parts the employee was materially
  involved in over the last 12 months), **Restricted Customer** and **Restricted Person** (12-month
  look-back, contact-based). Note that a blanket covenant applied equally to junior and senior staff
  is strongest for senior/commercial roles and weakest for junior ones.
- **Confidentiality** — Obligations addendum: no use/disclosure of Confidential Information during
  or after employment (usually indefinite), with standard carve-outs (authorised/legally required,
  public domain, protected disclosure under s.43A ERA 1996).
- **Notice Period** — give **both** sides. Standard is often 3 months each way after probation
  (1 week during probation), with summary dismissal for gross misconduct. Flag any unusual length
  (e.g. 6 months) or statutory sliding scales (common in apprenticeships).
- **Termination Conditions** — summary dismissal without notice/PILON for gross misconduct;
  disciplinary/appeal/grievance per the Employee Handbook. Note whether long-term sickness is
  addressed separately (often it isn't).
- **IP** — with the **employer**: IP in inventions/works vests automatically in the company on
  creation (held on trust if not), moral rights waived, power of attorney to perfect title.
- **Data protection** — a clause referring to the Employee Handbook and Privacy Notice. Some older
  templates only have the heading — note if the substantive wording is absent/illegible.
- **Governing Law** — often **not stated** in these documents; note "Not specified (England &
  Wales implied)" rather than asserting one.
- **Redundancy pay** — usually not addressed → statutory only. Note any enhancement.

## Variable fields (differ per person — always read these individually)

- **Date signed** — per document, give the company and employee signing dates and whether **both
  parties** signed. See Step 4 in SKILL.md — establish this **visually**, not from OCR.
- **Commencement date** — the date the person **started working**. Watch for two dates: the current
  contract's commencement (often a re-issue date when the employer re-papered contracts) and the
  **continuous employment date** (an earlier "period of continuous employment began on…"). The
  continuous-service date is the one that matters — it drives statutory redundancy, unfair-dismissal
  protection, statutory notice and service-related benefits. Lead with the real start date and note
  the re-issue date in brackets.
- **Job title** — the current title (after any variation); note the original if changed.
- **Salary** — current per-annum figure after the latest variation, with the effective date and the
  original in brackets. Flag large undocumented jumps or a promotion with no pay change.
- **Hours** — normal hours/days; flag part-time patterns and any hours variation.

## Comments / Outstanding points / Satisfactory

- **Comments** — a short plain-English summary of the key points for that employee.
- **Outstanding points** — what still needs checking (missing signature, illegible clause,
  undocumented change, apprenticeship funding/clawback for a leaver, etc.).
- **Satisfactory?** — a simple verdict. Use "Satisfactory" when the contract is complete and
  standard, and "Investigate" when something needs follow-up (unsigned/partly-executed, unusual
  terms, unexplained pay jump, invalid dates). Colour-code if the template expects it (green =
  satisfactory, amber = investigate).
