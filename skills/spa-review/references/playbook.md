# Tern Capital SPA Negotiation Playbook — Buy-Side Positions

This file is the definitive reference for Tern's positions when buying software businesses. Use it to draft responses to every seller issue. If a topic is not covered here, use reasonable buyer-friendly judgment and flag "Needs review".

---

## Limitations — Quantum

| Item | **Tern (Buyer) Position** | Seller Typical Ask |
|------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Cap — Fundamental Warranties | 100% Total Consideration | 100% Total Consideration |
| Cap — non-Fundamental (Business, Tax) Warranties | 100% Total Consideration | Often 50–75% |
| De-minimis | **0.05%** of Completion Consideration | 0.5% of Total Consideration |
| Basket | **0.25%** of Completion Consideration (tipping) | 2.0% of Total Consideration |

**Key talking points:**
- Sellers regularly push the basket to 2%; Tern will negotiate to a maximum of ~0.5% if needed, but open at 0.25%
- De-minimis should stay as low as possible — Tern's position is 0.05% CC
- Tern does NOT agree to a non-disclosure cap below 100% for business/tax warranties

## Limitations — Time Periods

| Warranty / Claim Type | **Tern Position** | Seller Typical Ask |
|----------------------|------------------|--------------------|
| Business Warranties | **48 months** from completion | 12–18 months |
| Tax Warranties | **7 years** | 3 years |
| Tax Indemnities | **7 years** | 3 years |
| Specific Indemnities | **3 years** | 1 year |
| Court proceedings (after notice of claim) | **12 months** | 3 months |

**Key talking points:**
- 24 months for business warranties is a minimum floor Tern might accept if pressed
- Tax periods are non-negotiable at 7 years (aligned with HMRC enquiry windows)
- The court proceedings window is important for protecting claim value — 6 months is a compromise; 3 months is too short

## Joint & Several vs Several Liability

| Claim Type | **Tern Position** |
|------------|-------------------|
| Fundamental Warranties | **Several** only (each seller liable for own %) |
| Restrictive Covenants | **Several** only |
| Business Warranties | **Joint & Several** |
| Tax Warranties | **Joint & Several** |
| Tax Indemnities | **Joint & Several** |
| Specific Indemnities | **Joint & Several** |
| Leakage — own | **Several** only |
| Leakage — third party | **Joint & Several** |

**Key talking points:**
- Sellers (especially minority shareholders) routinely push for several-only on all claims. Tern's position is joint & several on all warranty and indemnity claims except FW, RC, and own leakage.
- If a seller insists on several-only for BW, this should be escalated — it significantly weakens the buyer's position.

## Set-Off Against Deferred Consideration

- **Tern's position:** Tern retains the right to withhold (set off) any outstanding warranty or indemnity claims against deferred consideration / earn-out payments
- **Sellers' typical position:** Deferred consideration should be paid as it falls due; claims resolved separately in court
- **Tern's response:** Reject seller's position. Set-off is a key commercial protection. We will accept reasonable carve-outs for disputed amounts held in escrow, but set-off must remain.

---

## Awareness / Knowledge Qualifications

- **Tern's preferred position:** Warranties are given absolutely (no awareness qualification), OR at minimum qualified only by Seller's awareness after "careful and diligent enquiries" of senior management
- **Sellers' typical ask:** Qualified by "actual knowledge" (without duty of enquiry)
- **Tern's response to "actual knowledge without enquiry":** Reject. The standard must be "awareness after careful enquiries". We may accept this if Seller insists, but not "actual knowledge" alone.
- **Buyer's knowledge bar:** Tern will NOT accept buyer's knowledge as a bar to warranty claims. Sellers often argue that if the buyer knew about an issue in due diligence, they cannot claim — Tern rejects this.

---

## Disclosure Standard

- **Tern's position:** "Fairly disclosed" against the disclosure letter with specific reference to supporting documents
- **Sellers' typical ask:** "Generally disclosed" across the entire data room
- **Tern's response:** Reject blanket data room disclosure. Accept "fairly disclosed" with specific references. General disclosure may apply to clearly defined categories (e.g., publicly available documents), but not as a catch-all.

---

## Deferred Consideration — Security

- **Tern's position:** No security over deferred consideration. Sellers take unsecured credit risk.
- **Sellers' typical ask:** Cross-guarantees from parent/shareholders, security over target shares, or a converting share on default
- **Tern's response:** Reject security requests. Tern may offer comfort via strong set-off rights and regular reporting, but will not grant security.

---

## Locked Box

- **Profit Ticker:** Tern position is **no profit ticker** from the Locked Box Date. Sellers often ask for interest/ticker on the purchase price from the LBD to completion.
- **Leakage — joint & several:** Tern wants joint & several liability for third-party leakage. Own leakage is several only.
- **Leakage claim period:** **6 months** from completion (sellers push for 3 months)
- **Permitted leakage:** Must be specifically and exhaustively listed. Tern resists broad catch-all permitted leakage categories.

## Restrictive Covenants

All of the following apply to **ALL Sellers** (not just key persons):

| Covenant | **Tern Position** |
|----------|-------------------|
| Non-compete | **3 years** in the business's field |
| Non-solicitation of customers | **3 years** |
| Non-poaching of customers | **3 years** |
| Non-poaching of suppliers | **3 years** |
| Non-poaching of employees | **3 years** |

**Key talking points:**
- Sellers push for shorter periods (1–2 years) and narrower scope (key persons only)
- 2 years may be acceptable as a compromise if Seller raises specific concerns about particular shareholders
- Geographic scope should cover territories where the business operates

## Warranties

### Material Contracts
- **Tern position:** "Material contract" defined to capture contracts with counterparties representing **90%** of the business's supplier/customer spend or revenue
- **Sellers' ask:** 50–70% threshold

### Accounts
- **Tern position:** Accounts warranted as prepared in accordance with **UK accounting standards** (FRS 102 or IFRS), NOT "consistently with prior years" or "on a basis consistent with historic preparation"
- The "historic preparation methods" qualification allows sellers to bury accounting irregularities in past practice

---

## Tax

### R&D Tax Credits
- Any additional R&D tax credit obtained through Buyer's (post-completion) efforts or analysis belongs to Buyer, not to the Sellers under the tax covenant

### Tax Covenant Period
- 7 years for both tax warranties and tax indemnities (as above in Limitations)

## Earn-Outs (Schedule 11 / Deferred Consideration)

### General Principles
- Earn-outs bridge valuation gaps; typically 30–50% of EV is deferred (earn-out + fixed deferred)
- Earn-out period: 1–2 years typical; up to 3–5 years if sellers remain engaged
- Metrics: financial preferred (EBITDA, GP, revenue); non-financial as supplements only

### Cap and Collar
- **Cap:** Maximum earn-out payment must be defined — protects Tern from unlimited upside exposure
- **Collar:** Minimum performance threshold below which no earn-out triggers — protects Tern from paying if business underperforms
- EBITDA definitions must be tightly defined with agreed add-backs

### Seller Protections During Earn-Out
- Sellers will request "non-interference" undertakings (Tern cannot reduce EBITDA by charging management fees, group recharges, etc.) — Tern may accept reasonable restrictions but not open-ended undertakings
- Sellers may ask for input into operational decisions during earn-out — Tern resists veto rights but may accept "consultation" obligation

### Deferred Consideration — Set-Off
- As above: Tern has right to set off claims against deferred consideration payments

## Key Claim Definitions (for reference in responses)

- **FW** = Fundamental Warranties (title & capacity)
- **BW** = Business Warranty Claim
- **TW** = Tax Warranty Claim
- **TI** = Tax Indemnity Claim
- **SI** = Specific Indemnity Claim
- **Claim** = FW + BW + TW
- **Tax Claim** = TW + TI
- **CC** = Completion Consideration
- **TC** = Total Consideration (including deferred)
